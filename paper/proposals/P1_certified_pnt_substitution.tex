\proposalheader
  {P1 --- Certified PNT Substitution}
  {Which Navigation Source Certifies? Alternative PNT Under a Composed
   Network-to-Navigation Guarantee}
  {Research proposal, RobustUAVs.ai project $\cdot$ Roger Nick Anaedevha $\cdot$
   drafted 2026-08-13\\
   Follow-on to \emph{From Wire to Flight} (NDSS submission)}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The parent manuscript proves that a network detector's operating point
$\theta$ bounds a certified Mission-Completion-Rate floor, and finds
empirically that the decisive quantity is not the proof but the
\emph{interface}: $\gamma$, the metres of position error accrued per second of
degraded navigation input. This proposal observes that $\gamma$ is a property
of the \emph{navigation source}, not of the attack. Changing the
positioning--navigation--timing (PNT) modality therefore moves the certified
window without altering a line of the proof. We propose to measure $\gamma$ per
modality, to show that a cryptographically authenticated PNT source discharges
the re-anchoring hypothesis the parent theorem currently assumes, and to derive
from the certificate a sensor-selection rule: the minimal sensor suite whose
fused $\gamma$ keeps a given detector setting inside the certified window.
\end{abstract}

% =====================================================================
\section{The gap}
% =====================================================================
The parent manuscript certifies a GNSS-based navigation stack, and its
Limitations section states the boundary plainly: Theorem~1 relies on the
re-anchoring hypothesis, that at least one uncorrupted state update arrives in
every certification horizon $T_c$. A time-delay adversary satisfies this by
construction, because it postpones corrections without suppressing them. A
\emph{persistent-denial} adversary does not, and there the certified radius
degrades as $e^{L T_c}$ in the achieved denial duration.

That admission is the opening. An entire literature on GNSS-denied navigation
has spent a decade building precisely the capability that restores the
hypothesis, and none of it is expressed as a re-anchoring
guarantee.

Two facts make the instance concrete. First, Iridium's Satellite Time and
Location signal arrives at the receiver roughly three orders of magnitude
stronger than GNSS and carries cryptographic authentication, so a receiver can
\emph{reject} a spoofed or tampered navigation solution rather than fuse
it~\cite{insidegnss_iridium,groundcontrol_apnt}; STL-aided inertial units are
being productised for exactly this
purpose~\cite{nal_vectornav_stl}. Second, opportunistic navigation from
low-Earth-orbit signals is no longer speculative: multi-constellation work using
four Starlink, one OneWeb, two Orbcomm and one Iridium satellite reports
$9.5$\,m 3D RMSE and $4.4$\,m final 3D
error~\cite{insidegnss_multiconstellation,kassas2021leodoppler}, and
resilient navigation under jamming has been demonstrated on signals of
opportunity~\cite{kassas2022jammer}.

What nobody has asked is the composed question:

\begin{quote}
\itshape
Does switching PNT source move the detector's operating point into the certified
window, and by how much?
\end{quote}

\section{The claim}
% =====================================================================
Let a platform carry a set $S$ of PNT modalities --- GNSS, LEO signals of
opportunity, terrain-referenced navigation, visual--inertial odometry, and
inertial-only dead reckoning --- each characterised by

\begin{itemize}
\item a \textbf{degradation rate} $\gamma_s$, in metres of position error per
      second of stale or withheld input;
\item an \textbf{authentication state}
      $a_s\in\{\text{authenticated},\text{unauthenticated}\}$; and
\item an \textbf{availability process} over the mission.
\end{itemize}

\begin{claim}[$\gamma$ becomes a vector]
\label{cl:vector}
The certified window edge
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:window}
\theta^{*}(m) \;=\; \frac{m}{\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}\; e^{L T_c}\; H}
\end{equation}
is evaluated against the $\gamma$ of the \emph{fused} estimate, so admitting a
low-$\gamma$ source strictly widens the window. This is arithmetic rather than
new theory, and that is the point: the contribution is the \emph{measurement} of
$\gamma$ per modality, exactly as the parent paper's contribution was the
measurement of $\gamma$ for GNSS.
\end{claim}

\begin{claim}[Authentication discharges the re-anchoring hypothesis]
\label{cl:auth}
A cryptographically authenticated PNT source supplies an uncorrupted update per
$T_c$ \emph{even under RF denial of the unauthenticated sources}, because an
adversary can jam it but cannot forge it, and a jammed authenticated source is
detected rather than fused. This converts the parent manuscript's admitted
limitation into a hypothesis that is satisfiable, with named hardware behind it.
\end{claim}

\begin{claim}[The certificate induces a sensor-selection rule]
\label{cl:selection}
Given a corridor margin $m$, a detector operating point $\theta$, and a
per-modality cost, the certificate induces a partial order over sensor suites:
the minimal suite whose fused $\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}$ keeps $\theta$ inside the
window. This is a procurement-grade output, and it is what an operator actually
wants from a robustness certificate.
\end{claim}

\begin{honestly}
Claim~\ref{cl:auth} is the load-bearing one and also the one most exposed.
Authentication prevents \emph{forgery}; it does not prevent \emph{denial}. The
claim is therefore about the adversary's ability to make the estimator fuse a
lie, not about availability. If an adversary jams every modality at once, the
hypothesis fails again and the certificate is silent. The paper must say this in
the same breath as the claim.
\end{honestly}

\section{Experiment plan}
% =====================================================================
\subsection{Measuring $\gamma$ per modality}
GNSS is done: the parent manuscript reports $\gamma = 1.195$\,m/s at the
receiver and $1.365$\,m/s after the extended Kalman filter, calibrated on three
real PX4 flights~\cite{whelan2020uav,kong2024px4ekf}. The remainder, in
descending order of evidential strength:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Inertial-only.} Measurable \emph{today} from the same flight logs,
by holding the GNSS fix and integrating the inertial measurements. This yields
the dead-reckoning $\gamma$ that the parent paper's cruise discussion currently
\emph{asserts} from an error budget. Do this first: it retroactively strengthens
the manuscript's most contested paragraph, and the data is already staged.
\item \textbf{Visual--inertial.} Public VIO datasets with UAV motion profiles.
\item \textbf{LEO signals of opportunity.} The honest gap. Either a partnership
for STL receiver logs, or a \code{physics\_model} $\gamma$ derived from
published RMSE and update rate, tagged as such.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{honestly}
A modelled $\gamma$ must never be reported as a measured one. The parent
project's provenance discipline exists for exactly this case, and the ledger
will show the tag whether or not the prose does.
\end{honestly}

\subsection{Fusion model}
Begin with the conservative
$\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}} = \min_{s\,\in\,S_{\mathrm{avail}}} \gamma_s$ over
available authenticated sources, then the filter-weighted version. Report both:
the conservative one is what the certificate is entitled to, the weighted one is
what a deployment will actually see.

\subsection{Denial sweep}
Extend \code{experiments/sweep\_full.py} with a denial-duration axis, giving the
certified floor as a surface over $(\theta,\ \text{denial duration})$ for each
modality set. The interesting reading is where the surface stops falling: that
is the point at which authentication has taken over from GNSS.

\subsection{Verifying the selection rule}
Check the order induced by Claim~\ref{cl:selection} against brute force over all
suites. The rule is only worth publishing if it agrees.

\section{Risks}
% =====================================================================
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The LEO $\gamma$ may stay unmeasured.} Mitigation: the paper
stands on GNSS, inertial-only and visual--inertial, with LEO as a clearly
tagged modelled extension. Three measured modalities against the parent paper's
one is already the contribution.
\item \textbf{The fusion model could become the contribution by accident.} Keep
it conservative and unglamorous. The contribution is the measurement and the
rule, not the estimator.
\item \textbf{Authentication claims rest partly on vendor
sources}~\cite{groundcontrol_apnt}. Any security property attributed to STL must
be cited to a specification or an independent evaluation before it enters a
theorem statement.
\end{itemize}
