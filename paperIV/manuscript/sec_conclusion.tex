% ------------------------------------------------------------- Conclusion
A UAV's navigation stack cannot distinguish an attacker from the weather, and
an attacker who knows this will exploit it. We turned that observation into a
threat model, $\varepsilon$-observational equivalence, and a bound on undetected
mission failure for every coupling of the benign sources. In the reduced-order
study every disguise evaded every detector, and only a trusted re-anchoring fix
capped the undetected deviation of all of them. For an explicit adaptive GNSS
attacker that cap is now proved on the linear loop: it is finite only with the
fix and shrinks to \nBoundTaOne\,m at 1\,s, and PX4 SITL flights at that interval
stayed within \nPxTaWorstDOneGate\,m. The re-anchoring interval, not a threshold,
is therefore the defender's design parameter. Engineers can size it to the
corridor a mission must hold; certification authorities can ask for a bound on
undetected failure whose every condition is proved or monitored at run time,
not only a detection rate; and researchers gain a way to state and falsify such
bounds under pre-registered protocols. Bounding the mismatch between the linear
loop and EKF2 and adding the run-based detectors to the proof come next.
