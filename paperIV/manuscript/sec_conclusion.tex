% ------------------------------------------------------------- Conclusion
A UAV's navigation stack cannot distinguish an attacker from the weather, and
an attacker who knows this will exploit it. We turned that observation into a
threat model, $\varepsilon$-observational equivalence, and a bound on undetected
failure for every coupling of the benign sources. Optimised disguises evaded
every detector, and for an explicit adaptive GNSS attacker the undetected
deviation is now proved on the linear loop: finite only with a trusted
re-anchoring fix and \nBoundTaOne\,m at 1\,s, while PX4 SITL flights at that
interval stayed within \nPxTaWorstDOneGate\,m. The re-anchoring interval, not a threshold,
is therefore the defender's design parameter. Engineers can size it to the
corridor a mission must hold; certification authorities can require a bound on
undetected failure, with every condition proved or monitored, not only a
detection rate; and researchers gain a way to state and falsify such
bounds under pre-registered protocols. Bounding the mismatch to EKF2 and proving
the run-based detectors come next.
