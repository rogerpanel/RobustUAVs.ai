% ------------------------------------------------------------- Conclusion
A UAV's navigation stack cannot distinguish an attacker from the weather, and
an attacker who knows this will exploit it. We turned that observation into
a threat model, a requirement and a measurement. The threat model is
$\varepsilon$-observational equivalence, which bounds every detector's advantage
by a total-variation distance. The requirement is that a mission-level
guarantee must bound undetected failure without relying on the detector to
notice the disguise. The measurement is a cross-evaluated evasion study on a
cross-layer substrate with enforced provenance. In that study every disguise
evaded every detector, and the deviation a detector admitted depended on
whether it modelled the disguise as benign, unless a trusted re-anchoring
alarm capped it for all of them.

The unified budget meets the requirement. Its stochastic terms are aggregated
over every coupling, including one an attacker imposes by controlling when
jamming happens; its deterministic terms add no dependence gap; and its single
empirical adversarial input is reported together with the case in which an
independent attacker exceeded it. The analysis also locates the looseness in
the dependence, envelope and temporal gaps rather than in the union bound. In
our pilot the temporal gap was the largest at moderate budgets, and it is the
most tractable. For the defender, re-anchoring and closing the
terminal interval mattered more than any threshold.

The scaled test provides a second finding. The pilot's clean result did not
survive a larger test, and the violations it found came from benign missions
rather than attacks, from an unmonitored assumption and from a sample
asked to certify more than it could. Once both were
secured, the bound held on fresh data, although its adversarial term
remains measured rather than proved. The provable
alternative is \nREightSatRatio$\times$ looser, because the detectors that
actually constrain the attacker alarm on patterns that no convex bound
captures. Closing that gap, re-establishing the results in PX4
software-in-the-loop simulation, and calibrating every marginal on measured
flights are the next steps, as the artifact's README specifies.
