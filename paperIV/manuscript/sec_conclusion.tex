% ------------------------------------------------------------- Conclusion
A UAV's navigation stack cannot distinguish an attacker from the weather, and
an attacker who knows this will exploit it. We turned that observation into a
threat model, $\varepsilon$-observational equivalence; a requirement, that a
mission-level guarantee bound undetected failure without relying on the detector
to notice the disguise; and a bound that meets it for every coupling of the
benign sources. Every disguise evaded every detector, and only a trusted
re-anchoring fix capped the undetected deviation of all of them. For an explicit adaptive GNSS attacker that cap is now proved on the linear loop:
it is finite only with the fix and shrinks to \nBoundTaOne\,m at 1\,s, so the
re-anchoring interval, not a threshold, is the defender's design parameter.
After a scaled test exposed two unmonitored conditions, the repaired bound held
on fresh data, and PX4 SITL flights transferred the stealth budget but not the
full disguise matrix. Bounding the mismatch between the linear loop and EKF2 and
adding the run-based detectors to the proof come next.
