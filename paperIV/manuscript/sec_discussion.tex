\paragraph{Summary of findings.}
Every disguise evades every detector and only the anchor gate caps the
undetected deviation (RQ1); the repaired bound survived fresh attackers
(RQ2--RQ5); a measured GNSS marginal cut the floor two- to threefold (RQ6); in
PX4 the stealth budget, not the full disguise matrix, transferred, and 1\,s
re-anchoring held undetected deviation within \nPxTaWorstDOneGate\,m (RQ7).

\paragraph{Design rules for defenders.}
Five design rules follow from the measurements.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Re-anchor, and size the interval rather than the threshold.} With a
  trusted fix every 5\,s and its gate armed, frequent evasions contributed at
  most \nGatedCap\,m (\nNgMax\,m without the gate). Lengthening the interval
  from 5 to 20\,s multiplied the budget by \nAnchorMultTwenty{}
  (\cref{fig:anchor}), whereas moving from 1\% to 10\% FPR left it unchanged;
  the proved budget falls almost linearly with $T_a$ (\cref{tab:boundta}), and
  in PX4, 1\,s instead of 5\,s cut the worst undetected deviation from
  \nPxWorstAllGate{} to \nPxTaWorstAllGate\,m. For a corridor of margin $m$,
  choose $T_a$ with $\dadv(T_a)\le m-e^{LT_c}\dnet-s^\star$, where $s^\star$ is
  the smallest budget with $\pbar(s^\star)$ below the tolerated risk.
\item \emph{Close the last interval.} An unchecked final segment multiplied the
  budget by \nOpenRatioTwenty{} at $T_a=20$\,s, so a trusted fix matters most
  during landing and terminal approach.
\item \emph{Model the inertial bias.} Without the gate, only the detector whose
  filter models the accelerometer bias (D2) capped every disguise; D4 still
  admitted \nNgCapDFour\,m to the fault mimic.
\item \emph{Certify the undetected outcome, not the alarm.} \Cref{thm:floor}
  bounds $\Pr[\text{fail}\wedge U]$ without relying on any detector catching a
  particular disguise; a floor from benign marginals gated on silence does not
  (\cref{prop:gated}).
\item \emph{Alarm on what the certificate assumes.} The scaled test failed
  exactly where the loop saturated; a saturation alarm turns that assumption
  into a run-time check at a cost of \nRevSatAlone{} benign false alarms.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Reduced-order model.} The proof and the soundness test cover one
  lateral channel with a linear KF. The PX4 study (\cref{sec:px4}) ran in SITL,
  needed a small EKF2 change, and transferred the stealth budget but not the
  full disguise matrix; a model-mismatch term to EKF2 is not yet bounded.
\item \emph{Empirical adversarial term.} The usable $\dadv$ is a pooled supremum
  over optimised attackers; stronger optimisers raised it by
  \nROneDadvRatioFive$\times$, and a held-out mixed-family attacker exceeded it
  (\nROneAdvMax\,m). The proved budget (\cref{sec:certbudget}) omits the
  run-based detectors D2 and D4; a white-box gradient attacker is future work.
\item \emph{Finite calibration and power.} With \nCalMissions{} samples per
  source the band certifies at most $\nRFiveFloorCpTwenty$, and where the
  revised bound is informative no undetected failure occurred, so it has not
  been stressed near its value (the negative controls show the test can fail).
\item \emph{Partly simulated inputs.} Only the GNSS marginal is measured
  (\cref{sec:r5}; one campaign, one airframe), and $\dnet$ enters analytically
  as the tube of \cref{sec:background}.
\item \emph{Covert attackers, no IMU injection.} An attacker indifferent to
  detection is bounded by detection latency and the failsafe, which we do not
  certify; one who can also inject into the IMU
  (acoustically~\cite{son2015,trippel2017} or via bus
  reconfiguration~\cite{erba2025}) defeats the inertial cross-checks, leaving
  only condition C2$'$.
\end{itemize}
