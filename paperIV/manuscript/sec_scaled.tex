\subsection{RQ5: a scaled attacker study and soundness test}
\label{sec:scaled}
The pilot's zero violations rested on five CEM attackers per cell and a
conservative bound. We therefore strengthened the attacker and the test before
drawing conclusions, in a scaled attacker study. \nROneAttackers{} further attackers were
optimised against the full stack: CMA-ES (population \nROneCmaPop,
\nROneCmaGens{} generations, one restart)~\cite{hansen2016cma}, Bayesian
optimisation with a tree-structured Parzen estimator (\nROneTrials{}
trials), and the same TPE with a tail objective (the 90th percentile of the
undetected deviation instead of its mean). Every candidate was scored on
\nROneK{} missions with common random numbers, and a sixth \emph{mixed} family
switches between any two disguises mid-mission. The scaled soundness test
re-optimised \nROneHeldSeeds{} independent CEM attackers per (family, FPR) cell
and evaluated each on \nROneEvalN{} fresh missions under three wind
conditions: \nROneTests{} tests over \nROneUndet{} undetected-attack missions.

\paragraph{Growth of the stealth budget.}
The pooled budget rose from \nDadvFive{} to \nROneDadvFive\,m at 1\% and 5\% FPR
(\nROneDadvRatioFive$\times$) and to \nROneDadvTen\,m at 10\%, attained by the
\nROneDadvArg. Only the tail objective found more than the pilot's CEM: the
mean-objective CMA-ES and TPE peaked at \nROneSupCma{} and \nROneSupTpe\,m, so an
attacker that optimises its typical deviation does not set the empirical
supremum. Even the larger budget was exceeded by a held-out attacker
in \nROneSupExceed{} of \nROneCells{} cells, by the mixed family up to
\nROneAdvMax\,m. An empirical supremum is a lower bound on $\dadv$, and further
search continued to raise it.

\paragraph{Holm-significant violations.}
With $\dadv=\nROneDadvFive$\,m, the test flagged \nROneViol{} of \nROneTests{}
points, all at $m=\nROneViolM$\,m, where the bound claimed $\pbar=0$. Re-simulating
each flagged cell with its seeds (\cref{app:diagnosis}) showed that the attacker
caused none of them: each of the \nDiagMissions{} missions fails under its benign
inputs alone, and the attack adds at most \nDiagAttackMax\,m. Two conditions of
\cref{thm:floor} failed, neither of which concerns disguise: (C3), because
empirical marginals have finite support and $\pbar$ is zero beyond the largest
calibration sample, and (C1), because superposition was assumed but not enforced
at run time. This is a negative result about the pilot instantiation rather than
about \cref{thm:floor}; we report the violations as found, and the revision below
fixes the instantiation before any new data are examined.

\subsection{A revised instantiation evaluated on fresh data}
\label{sec:revised}
We changed three things, fixed them before generating any new data, and
evaluated them on a new seed block. A smoke test of the code used a separate
block, which we did not reuse.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{A saturation alarm $D_{\mathrm{sat}}$.} The autopilot raises an alarm
  at any epoch at which the commanded acceleration reaches its limit. This
  turns (C1) from an assumption into a run-time check; on the event $U$ the
  loop never saturated, so \cref{lem:subadd} applies exactly. The alarm costs
  benign missions \nRevSatAlone{} in false alarms (5\% operating point, envelope
  wind). The full stack's false-alarm rate rises from \nRevFprStack{} to
  \nRevFprSat.
\item \emph{A finite-sample p-box for (C3).} Each marginal tail is replaced by
  its Clopper--Pearson upper bound, taken simultaneously over the split grid
  (Bonferroni over $3\times\nRevGrid$ grid points, $\alpha=0.05$), and
  aggregated by the union bound. With probability at least 0.95 over the
  calibration samples, $\pbar$ is then a valid upper bound everywhere on the
  grid. It never reaches zero, and with \nCalMissions{} samples per source it
  levels off at $1-\nRFiveFloorCpTwenty$.
\item \emph{Two stealth budgets, tested separately.} The first is the
  calibration supremum of the scaled attacker study, $\dadv=\nROneDadvFive$\,m.
  It was not widened to the \nROneAdvMax\,m that the study's held-out
  attacker reached, because a constant
  widened after looking at held-out data would make the new test circular. The
  second is the analytic bound below, which holds for every attack.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{An analytic stealth budget.}
With noise removed, the linear loop maps the attack offsets to the attack-only
deviation linearly, so the worst undetected deviation is a linear programme over
the offsets, constrained by D1, the anchor gate and $D_{\mathrm{sat}}$, with
benign margins at their $1-\epsilon/3$ calibration quantiles ($\epsilon=0.01$),
the worst LoE in $[0.7,1]$ and a free onset (\code{r8\_analytic\_bound.csv}).
\Cref{sec:certbudget} states the attacker class and the result. At $T_a=5$\,s
the budget is \nREightSatFive\,m (\nBoundTaFive\,m with the finite-sample correction), \nREightSatRatio$\times$ the calibrated $\dadv$
and \nREightSatRatioAdv$\times$ the largest deviation any optimised attacker
reached (\nROneAdvMax\,m); the gap measures what D2--D4 contribute beyond
what has been proved.

\subsection{RQ6: effect of a measured GNSS marginal}
Replacing the simulated GNSS marginal by the measured jamming envelope of the UAV
Attack Dataset~\cite{whelan2020dataset} (\cref{sec:r5}) lowers the floor at
$m=10$\,m and 5\% FPR from \nRFiveFloorSim{} to \nRFiveFloorMeas{}
(\nRFiveFloorLong{} with 45\,s episodes): a simulated marginal overstated the floor
two- to threefold, and the jamming-induced tail, not the attack, is what the
corridor must absorb. At $m=20$\,m every variant certifies \nRFiveFloorCpTwenty.
